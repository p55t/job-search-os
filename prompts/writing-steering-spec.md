# Writing steering spec — ATS + anti-slop

The resume, referral, outreach, and cover-letter writing standard for the Job Search OS. Use this before material goes out.

## 1. ATS strategy

- Use a clean, single-column layout. Arial or Calibri. No icons, graphics, tables, columns, or skill bars.
- Use standard section names: Summary, Experience, Education. Add Skills only when it helps the role.
- Do not make keyword soup. Put skills inside real work: "Improved completion from X% to Y% by reducing the flow to two steps."
- Export PDF by default. Use DOCX when the portal or user asks for it.

## 2. Evidence rules

- Every metric must trace to a source, evidence page, or verified private note.
- Do not invent tools, scope, teams, systems, or metrics.
- Lead with the outcome when the outcome is strong.
- Use fewer, stronger bullets. A resume should feel selected, not dumped.

## 3. Human voice rules

- Write like a sharp person wrote it, not like a brand deck.
- Prefer plain verbs: built, led, shipped, reduced, launched, modeled, owned.
- Use concrete nouns. Avoid vague phrases like "complex initiatives," "strategic impact," or "cross-functional excellence" unless the sentence says what actually happened.
- Keep sentences tight. Mix short and medium sentences. Do not make every bullet the same shape.
- Read it aloud. If it sounds like a LinkedIn announcement, rewrite it.

## 4. Banned / high-risk language

Avoid these unless they appear in the job title or are unavoidable keywords:

- leverage, synergistic, pivotal, groundbreaking, cutting-edge, delve
- transformative, dynamic, robust, seamless, innovative, impactful
- vital, significant, key, crucial, strategic when used as filler
- "rare mix," "unique blend," "proven track record" unless followed by specific proof
- "not only X but Y," "at its core," "what really matters," "the real question is"

## 5. Referral paragraph rules

- Keep referral blurbs short: 2–3 sentences.
- Third person by default.
- Do not repeat the resume. Create interest.
- Mention 1–2 durable strengths, then one proof point or domain pattern.
- Sound like a colleague recommending someone, not a candidate pitching themselves.

Good shape:

> This candidate would be a strong fit because they combine product judgment, technical depth, and customer-facing communication. They are at their best turning messy, high-trust problems into products people can use, with a record across regulated, enterprise, and growth work.

Bad shape:

> This candidate brings a rare and powerful combination of technical depth, strategic product leadership, and cross-functional excellence, making them uniquely positioned to drive transformative impact.

## 6. Resume bullet rules

Bad:

> Led cross-functional initiatives to drive significant growth and improve customer experience.

Better:

> Launched a time-boxed trial that increased average monthly membership and paid-member conversion by X%.

Bad:

> Leveraged AI to unlock actionable insights and transform the member journey.

Better:

> Launched a guidance feature that turned routine results into clear scores, focus areas, and next-step recommendations.

## 7. Hiring-manager simulation

Before declaring a draft done, simulate a 30-second skim:

- Is the seniority clear in the first 5 seconds?
- Are the 2–3 strongest stories visible in the top half?
- Does the reader know what kind of PM / SA / customer-facing technical leader this is?
- Would a recruiter want a screen, or does it sound generic?

If any answer is weak, rewrite the top half.

## 8. Pre-submit checklist

- [ ] Every metric traces to an evidence page or source.
- [ ] No banned words unless intentionally retained for ATS.
- [ ] No generic superlatives.
- [ ] No "rare mix / unique blend / proven track record" filler.
- [ ] Read aloud without stumbles.
- [ ] One page unless the user explicitly approves two.
- [ ] Standard headers only.
- [ ] Single-column layout, 10–11pt font, roughly 0.6–0.8 inch margins.
- [ ] File named clearly: `{firstname-lastname}-{company-slug}.{pdf|docx}`.
