# Anti-Slop & Human-Voice Spec

How to write so the result sounds like a person, not a content farm. Applies to resumes, outreach, interview prep, anything the OS generates that a human will read.

## 1. The "Human Voice" audit

- **Read aloud.** If you stumble over a sentence aloud, a recruiter will stumble over it in their head. Read every line at human speed.
- **No "resume persona."** Write as if you're talking to a peer, not a faceless hiring committee.
- **Action over being.** Delete "Responsible for," "Tasked with," "In charge of." Every bullet starts with a verb denoting completed work: "Launched," "Built," "Reduced," "Generated."
- **Kill hyperbole.** If an adjective ("innovative," "world-class") doesn't change the meaning or provide data, kill it.

## 2. AI-proofing (ATS + human readability)

- **Semantic clarity.** ATS algorithms look for skills in context. Don't hide skills in a keyword soup list. Use them in your bullets: "Scaled X using Y (Python) to achieve Z."
- **Formatting.** Keep it boring but clean. Standard headers, standard fonts. Avoid fancy icons, "skill bars," graphical elements that break parsers.
- **Density.** Don't waste space on "Objectives" or "Core Competencies" sections unless you have > 10 years experience. Your experience is your competency.

## 3. The "human-only" difference — the informational layer

- **Uniqueness matters.** Every worker can "lead projects." Only your work has the specific configuration of: *the exact problem, the exact constraint you broke through, the exact outcome.* Write down that configuration.
- **Specificity over generality.** "Reduced enrollment from 5 steps to 2" beats "improved enrollment flow." "Led 7-person team across clinical, legal, eng" beats "led cross-functional team."
- **Messy problem rule.** A generic bullet says "Led cross-functional initiatives." A human bullet says: "Led 7-person team. Blocked by clinical compliance; redesigned workflow to meet [constraint], launched in 4 months."

## 4. Sentence shape

- Mix very short, punchy sentences (3–6 words) with medium-length compound sentences.
- Avoid long, comma-laden run-ons that read like LLM output.
- One idea per sentence.

## 5. Evidence-first

- Metrics (%, $, bps, #) at the start or core of the bullet.
- No editorializing — never say something was "impressive," "vital," or "significant." Present the data; let the reader judge.

## 6. The banned words

Never use:

leverage · synergistic · pivotal · groundbreaking · delve · cutting-edge · world-class · innovative (adj.) · Responsible for · Tasked with · In charge of · Successfully (just describe the outcome) · Helped to (you either did or you didn't)
